# Davidson Fellows Scholarship: is it worth applying with Serein? (draft, 2026-11-08)

Awards of $25,000, $50,000 or $100,000 for "a significant piece of work" by students 18
and under. Deadline **February 17, 2027, 11:59 pm Pacific**; decisions by July 15.
Source: https://www.davidsongifted.org/gifted-programs/fellows-scholarship/eligibility/how-to-apply/

## Two constraints to settle before anyone starts

1. **Team size.** Davidson accepts individuals or **two-person teams only**, and a
   two-person team must email DavidsonFellows@DavidsonGifted.org *before* starting the
   application. Serein has four people. The honest options: (a) the two members whose
   work is most separable (for example, SmokeCast and its evaluation) apply as a team
   with a clearly scoped project, crediting the rest of the team in the submission;
   (b) nobody applies; (c) one member applies alone with their own component. Option
   (a) only works if the other two agree in writing beforehand; a scholarship that
   splits a team three months before the Summit is not worth it.
2. **Residency.** Historically the program requires US citizens or permanent residents
   residing in the US (check the Eligibility & FAQs page; it was not on the how-to-apply
   page). Confirm for each would-be applicant.

Other rules from the page: one project per applicant; "community service projects are
not accepted"; no Google Docs or Google-hosted files; YouTube videos must be unlisted;
one parent approval form per team member; two nominators.

## If the team goes ahead: what maps from the Conrad work

Davidson's categories include Science, Technology, Engineering and Mathematics; the
submission is judged as research, not as a business. That means the pieces Conrad
weights least are the pieces Davidson weights most, which is why the two applications
reinforce each other rather than compete.

| Davidson component (confirm exact list in the category requirements) | What we already have | What is missing |
|---|---|---|
| Project description / abstract | Brief Q1, Q4; README "How it works" | Rewrite for a scientific reader; drop pricing |
| Research paper or technical report | `docs/METHODS.md`, `docs/BACKTEST_PROTOCOL.md`, `docs/results/dev_report.md`, the final test report | Assemble into one document with methods, results, limitations, references (sections A and B of `references_DRAFT.md`) |
| Process / "how I did it" essay | Change log in the protocol; the repeatable-training story (Q-7); the decision to report where we lose | Write in first person; name the specific failures and what they taught |
| Significance / impact essay | Brief Q3; the persistence-baseline argument (BAMS 2026) | Frame as a contribution to forecast evaluation practice, not as a product |
| Nominating forms (two) | Coach; clinician reviewer (after C-8) or a scientist who has seen the protocol | Ask by mid-January; send them the final report |
| Video (if required by category) | Innovation Video footage | A research-oriented cut: the method, the test, the result |
| Supplementary materials | GitHub repository, figures | Tag a release at the final-test commit so the reviewers see a frozen artifact |

## Timeline that does not collide with Conrad

- Dec 7: final test runs (D-5). This is the result the Davidson paper needs.
- Jan 7: Conrad submission. Nothing Davidson-specific before this date.
- Jan 8 to Feb 10: assemble the technical report from existing docs; write the two
  essays; collect nominations and parent forms.
- Feb 17: submit.

## Recommendation

Decide the team-size question at the next meeting. If the answer is (a), email Davidson
in January to register the two-person team, and treat the technical report as a
by-product of writing up the final test, which the team has to do anyway.
