# Who to ask for an interview (task E-1)

Written 2026-11-26. The [interview guide](INTERVIEW_GUIDE.md) says what to ask. This
file says who to ask first, how to reach them, and what to send. Every organization
below was read on its own public page on 2026-11-25 or 2026-11-26; the sources are at
the end. Nobody on this list has been contacted, and nothing here means they will say
yes.

Target: 5 parents, 2 school nurses, 1 pediatric clinician. Send the first six asks on
Monday, November 30 (this week is Thanksgiving). Expect one reply for every three or
four asks, so send more than you need.

## Rules for this list

- This repository is public. It lists organizations, job titles and public office
  contacts only. Do not add a person's name, personal email or mobile number here.
- Reach people through the page or number given. If a page lists a named contact, use
  it in your email, not in this file.
- You are a minor writing to adults at work. Copy our coach or a parent on every email
  and say so in the first line. [TEAM: name the coach before the first email goes out.]
- Parents are reached only through someone they already trust (a nurse, a program, a
  coach) forwarding a short note. Do not post in a parent group without asking its
  moderators first, and never ask a program for names.
- After each call, save notes as `P1_YYYY-MM-DD.md`, `N1_...` or `C1_...` with no names,
  as the guide says.

## What their own documents already say

Read these before a call with a nurse or district. They make the questions specific.

- The San Ramon Valley chart and the air district chart both act on AQI bands. At
  101 to 150 the San Ramon Valley chart says indoor lunch and recess are "strongly
  recommended for those with asthma or heart disease"; at 201 and above, "No outdoor
  activity."
- Neither chart says whether a school should use the AQI right now or a forecast.
- Neither says who decides. The San Ramon Valley chart names someone only for athletic
  events, where the host school "determines AQI using an app or website" and AirNow
  is the site named.
- The air district's chart sends schools to baaqmd.gov, and says districts "may
  consider closures based on site-by-site concerns."
- Pleasanton USD's medication page lets students carry asthma inhalers with a signed
  contract. Its emergency page does not mention air quality.

Three questions that follow, to add to the guide's list for nurses and districts:

1. "Your chart acts on the AQI at the time. Who looks, when, and who makes the call?"
2. "If you knew by early afternoon that tomorrow would be 101 to 150 at your school,
   what would you do differently, if anything?"
3. "On a smoke day, how does the health office know which students have asthma?"

Write down the answer to question 2 word for word. It decides whether a school
district is the right first customer (business plan, section 8).

## What to send

Three sentences, then stop. Change the middle sentence for each reader. Put the person's
name and yours in the email, not here.

**To a school nurse or district health office**

> Hello, I'm a student at Foothill High School in Pleasanton, writing with our coach
> copied. Our team is building a free tool that gives a school a wildfire smoke forecast
> for its own neighborhood a day ahead, and we want to learn how you decide what to do
> on a smoke day before we build any further. Could I ask you about ten questions in
> 15 minutes by phone or video in the next two weeks? No names are recorded.

**To an asthma program or educator**

> Hello, I'm a student at Foothill High School in Pleasanton, writing with our coach
> copied. Our team is building a free tool that turns tomorrow's smoke forecast into a
> plan for a child with asthma, and we would like to learn how families you work with
> get ready for smoke days now. Could one of your educators spare 15 minutes by phone
> or video in the next two weeks? No names are recorded, and we are not asking for any
> client information.

**To a clinician**

> Hello, I'm a student at Foothill High School in Pleasanton, writing with our coach
> copied. Our team is building a free tool that turns tomorrow's wildfire smoke
> forecast into a plan for a child with asthma, which always tells the family to follow
> their own doctor's action plan. Could I ask you about ten questions in 15 minutes
> about what you tell families on smoke days? No patient information is involved.

**To the air district**

> Hello, I'm a student at Foothill High School in Pleasanton, writing with our coach
> copied. Our team has built a neighborhood wildfire smoke forecast, scored against
> NOAA's and EPA's forecasts, and we would like to understand how schools use your
> School Air Quality Activity Recommendations on smoke days. Could someone in Community
> Engagement spare 15 minutes by phone or video in the next two weeks?

**A note for a nurse or program to forward to parents**

> Students at Foothill High School are building a free tool to help families of children
> with asthma get ready for wildfire smoke days. They would like to ask a few parents
> about ten questions in 15 minutes, by phone or video. No names are recorded and nothing
> is sold. If you are willing, reply to [team email address].
