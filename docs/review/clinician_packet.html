Serein: smoke-day asthma plans for review

Student project for the Conrad Challenge (Foothill High School, Pleasanton, CA). Generated from the code on the date of printing. Maya is a made-up child and the numbers are illustrative. Nothing here has been sent to a real family.

What we are asking

About 20 minutes, in writing. The five questions are on the last page; "fine" is a complete answer. We will make every change you ask for or tell you why not.

What Serein does

It forecasts wildfire smoke (daily average PM2.5) for a family's neighborhood one to three days ahead, with a range. For a child with asthma it turns tomorrow's forecast into a green, yellow or red plan the evening before, in English or Spanish, by text message with a link to the full plan. It never diagnoses, never changes medication, and always defers to the child's own asthma action plan.

How the zone is chosen

RuleZone
Forecast median "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" or worse (PM2.5 at or above 35.5 µg/m³), or the upper end of the forecast range "Unhealthy" or worseat least YELLOW
Forecast median "Very Unhealthy" or worseRED
The child's own log: estimated chance of symptoms tomorrow at least 1.3 times, or 5 percentage points above, the same child on a clean-air dayat least YELLOW
The same, at least 2 times or 15 points aboveRED

The child's log can only make a plan stricter than the public guidance, never looser. The 1.3x / 5-point and 2x / 15-point cut-offs are our design choices and the main thing we would like a clinician to judge. Only the smoke differs between "tomorrow" and the clean-air comparison, so a cold or a high-pollen day by itself does not trigger a smoke warning.

The evening check-in (six taps)

  1. Any asthma symptoms today? none / mild / moderate / severe
  2. Rescue (quick-relief) inhaler puffs today, not counting before exercise? 0 / 1-2 / 3-4 / 5+
  3. Woke up at night because of asthma? yes / no
  4. Time outdoors today? little (under 1 h) / some (1-3 h) / a lot (3 h+)
  5. Has a cold or is sick? yes / no
  6. Took daily controller medicine? yes / no / doesn't have one

A "symptom day", the outcome the model learns, is any symptoms, any rescue puffs, or night waking. The model is a Bayesian logistic regression that starts from published effect sizes (next section) and updates them for each child as days are logged. So far it has been tested only on 300 simulated children.

  1. Safety floor. Plans can only be stricter than EPA's guidance for sensitive groups, always say to follow the child's asthma action plan, and list emergency signs. Is anything missing?
