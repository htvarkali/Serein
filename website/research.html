The forecast model

SmokeCast

A local view of how smoke may move over the next three days.

SmokeCast uses readings from air monitors to refine NOAA forecasts. It estimates fine particle pollution, called PM2.5, and gives a range of possible values for each day.

How it works

From nearby readings to a local forecast.

SmokeCast brings air quality observations, weather forecasts, and satellite smoke information into one model.

Read the methods ↗

  1. Begin with NOAA forecasts

    The model starts with an available pollution forecast and learns how to adjust it using recent observations.

  2. Connect nearby monitors

    A graph neural network lets each location use information from nearby stations. The model trains with some readings hidden so it can estimate pollution between monitors.

  3. Show the uncertainty

    The model estimates a central value and a range. Calibration measures how often observations fall inside those ranges.

Evaluation

What remains to test.

The published figures come from a model trained on 2021–22 data and scored on held-out weeks in 2023. Later pipeline changes require a new development run. The separate 2024–25 evaluation is pending.

In a separate next-day warning comparison, 244 reporting-area days reached Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups or worse. SmokeCast flagged 173 of those (71%) and the official EPA reporting-area forecast flagged 112 (46%).
